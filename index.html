Dr. Trey Li

Research Fellow in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Manchester.

Email: trey (dot) li (at) manchester (dot) ac (dot) uk

I am a mathematician working on arithmetic geometry and quantum computing, with interests in cryptography and mathematical physics.

I received my PhD in Mathematics from the University of Auckland, where I worked under the supervision of Steven Galbraith.

After my PhD, I held research and teaching roles in the Department of Mathematics at the University of Auckland, before joining the University of Manchester.

My work has included mathematical foundations for post-quantum cryptography. In this line of work, I introduced the Hecke problem, the noisy subset product problem, and unknown-coefficient multivariate exponential equations.

I have also introduced unknowable quantum communication, the nonintrinsic sector of Landau theory, and continuum free-energy computing.

Activities

I am the organiser of the Manchester Interdisciplinary Seminar connecting mathematics, physics, and computer science, with a particular focus on security and cryptography. I also co-organise UK Crypto Day and UK Isogeny Day.
